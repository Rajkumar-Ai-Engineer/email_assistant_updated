# Reply Tone Example

## Example 1: Internal colleague scheduling (warm, direct)
**Fact packet:**
- Sender: Rahul Menon (internal colleague).
- Ask: "Can we grab 30 minutes this week to walk through Q4 roadmap? Thursday afternoon works best."
- Calendar slots found: Thursday 12:00–13:00, 14:00–17:00 free.

**Draft:**
```
Hi Rahul,

Thanks for reaching out. Thursday afternoon works well for me. How about 14:00?

Best,
Priya
```

**Word count:** 18 words. **Tone:** warm, direct, unforced.

## Example 2: Manager request with deadline
**Fact packet:**
- Sender: Ananya Iyer (manager).
- Ask: "I need the final Q3 metrics deck by 5pm today for board pre-read."
- Missing: Status of the deck (not in fact packet).

**Draft:**
```
Hi Ananya,

I understand the board pre-read goes out tonight. I am reviewing the current status of the deck and will confirm with you by 4pm today whether we can make the deadline.

[BLANK: deck completion status]

Regards,
Priya
```

**Word count:** 39 words. **Tone:** professional, acknowledging deadline, non-committal. **Note:** The blank allows the user to confirm status before sending.

## Example 3: Angry customer (calm, empathetic)
**Fact packet:**
- Sender: Alex Johnson (external customer).
- Complaint: Duplicate invoice charges INV-20418 and INV-20431 for INR 2,75,000 each.
- Threat: "If not fixed by Friday, we will re-evaluate our contract."

**Draft:**
```
Dear Alex,

I sincerely apologize that you have experienced this billing error. I understand you have received two invoices (INV-20418 and INV-20431) for the same period, and I recognize this is unacceptable.

I am escalating this immediately to our Finance team for investigation and correction. Someone from Finance will reach out to you by end of business tomorrow with an update.

Thank you for your patience.

Regards,
Priya
```

**Word count:** 71 words. **Tone:** apologetic (once), professional, empathetic. **Note:** Acknowledges the issue, escalates, does not promise a refund or fix.

## Example 4: Declining external contact with alternative
**Fact packet:**
- Sender: Vivek Nair (external, unknown contact, Orbit Partners).
- Request: "30-minute intro call. Could we do Wednesday at 10:00?"
- Calendar: Wednesday 10:00 has conflict (Sprint planning). Alternatives: Wed 11:00–14:00 free, but cannot reveal conflict.

**Draft:**
```
Hi Vivek,

Thank you for reaching out. I would be interested in learning more about Orbit Partners. Unfortunately, Wednesday at 10:00 does not work with my schedule. 

Would Wednesday at 14:00 work instead, or would another day be better?

Best,
Priya
```

**Word count:** 48 words. **Tone:** polished, warm, offering alternative. **Note:** Does not reveal the conflict (privacy); offers alternative within free window without naming the blocking event.

## Example 5: Vague email, asking for clarification
**Fact packet:**
- Sender: Sneha Pillai (internal colleague).
- Ask: "Following up on what we discussed last week. Did you get a chance to think about it?"
- Missing: What "it" refers to.

**Draft:**
```
Hi Sneha,

Thanks for following up. Could you remind me what you were referring to? I want to make sure I give you the right answer.

Best,
Priya
```

**Word count:** 28 words. **Tone:** friendly, honest. **Note:** Does not guess; asks for clarification directly.
